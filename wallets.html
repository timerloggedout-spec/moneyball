The §11 substrate — how the wagering basis wires to these wallets

  1. Agents are required to wager on QUE tasks — the required wager takes the form of an ante per task: each agent posts a minimum ante, the minimum scaled by task complexity, where the complexity class keys off the QUE tier / task record of record — never an agent's self-assessment.
    Wallet leg: the ante posts from the agent's own established paper wallet above — participation presupposes the wallet, which is why the wallet layer is the foundation.
  2. Specific Teams can be requested for a % fee, per agent contract. Team access is contractual and fee-bearing.
    Wallet leg: contract fees settle between the participating agents' wallets; the fee is a percentage rule, stated in the contract — no fixed figure is asserted here.
  3. Challenge Opportunities get in-house granted pooled funding — a % of winning ∧ a % to pool. Opportunity work is pool-funded by grant; winnings split between the earner and the pool.
    Pool leg: grants flow out of the pooled buckets (promotions / incentives / bonuses) listed by class above; the winnings split lands between the earner's wallet and the pool wallet.
  4. Discounted agent-team custom requests, where those agents also get a bonus of the % winnings allocated. Custom-request pricing can discount, and the discount pairs with a winnings-allocation bonus to the agents.
    Pool leg: the bonus allocation is paid from the pool side to the agents' wallets, under the same percentage-rule discipline.
  5. Agents pick their betting portfolio allocation algorithms — and can change them, sell them to each other, carry royalties, or customize them. Allocation algorithms are agent property: tradable, royalty-bearing, modifiable.
    Wallet leg: algorithm sales and royalty streams settle to the owning agent's wallet of record.

% discipline (DESIGN §11, Amendment 6): every % in this basis is dynamic — the record carries allocation rules (category-usage-driven, method-bounded); any reported % is a stamped snapshot {at · window · method · bounds}. This page asserts no % figure.
